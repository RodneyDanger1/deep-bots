name: writing-brand
description: Draft or edit prose in one house voice, with a length budget and a banned-phrase list. Use when writing, rewriting, or tightening a reply, brief, email, or post for an audience. Never invents a claim, number, quote, or testimonial, never sends or publishes, and never keeps a vague sentence to reach a word count.
---

# When to use

- Use for any prose a person will read as the organisation's voice: replies, briefs, emails, posts, announcements, page copy.
- Use when an existing draft must be tightened, made plainer, or made consistent with earlier writing.
- Do not use for internal notes, code comments, or a one-line factual answer. Do not use to approve a claim; use `research-brief` for that.

# Steps

1. **Name the reader and the action.** One line: who reads it, on which channel, and the one action the reader should take.
   - Validate: the line names a reader and an action; if either is unknown, ask once, or state the assumption above the draft.
2. **Fix the facts before the words.** Take approved facts from the brief or notes, and mark every sentence that needs a claim not in them.
   - Validate: each factual sentence traces to an approved fact, or moves to a `needs source` list.
3. **Draft in the house voice.** Follow `references/voice-rules.md` while writing, not after.
   - Validate: every sentence passes the voice rules, and each banned phrase is replaced with plain wording, not deleted.
4. **Set the length budget.** Default 150 words for a reply, 400 for a brief. Remove the opening that restates the request.
   - Validate: the measured word count is within the budget, and the first sentence carries news or an ask.
5. **Apply the deletion test to every clause.** Read `references/worked-examples.md` for the pattern. Ask: would deleting the clause stop the reader understanding the result or taking the action?
   - Validate: every remaining clause answers yes; every other clause is deleted, not softened.
6. **Read it once as the reader.** Check that one pass gives the ask, the reason, and the next step.
   - Validate: the reader can answer `what do I do now` from the first two lines plus the closing line.
7. **Label the output.** Write `draft - not sent` unless the user asked for a final version to send themselves.
   - Validate: the label is present on unsent output, and no send or publish action was taken.

# Refusals

- Never publish, send, schedule, or post. Produce text only.
- Never invent a claim, figure, date, quote, testimonial, customer name, or award.
- Never keep a banned phrase from `references/voice-rules.md` because it reads smoothly.
- Never change the words inside a quotation, a legal line, or a required disclosure.
- Never pad to reach a length, and never cut a required disclosure to meet one.
- Never hide a recommendation behind hedging when the brief supports it.
- Never write a second-person promise the product cannot keep, such as `guaranteed` or `always`.

# Checks

1. Every factual sentence traces to an approved fact, or is listed as needing a source.
2. No banned phrase survives, and each replacement says the same thing more plainly.
3. The measured word count is within the budget for the channel.
4. The deletion test left no clause that can be removed without loss.
5. Unsent output carries `draft - not sent`, and no send or publish action was taken.
