# Source tiers

Use these tiers to decide what a claim may rest on. Tier is about who wrote the page and whether the
page is the origin of the fact, not about whether the page agrees with the draft.

| Tier | What it is | May support a decision claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The origin itself: the organisation's own filing, standard, changelog, terms, dataset, court record, or the author's own published paper | Yes |
| 2 | Named-author reporting in an established outlet, peer-reviewed paper, recognised reference work, regulator summary | Yes |
| 3 | Aggregator, wiki, vendor blog, forum, social post, newsletter summary | No, as a lead only |
| 4 | Unattributed listicle, content farm, screenshot with no origin, AI-generated summary page | No |

## Rules

1. A decision-grade claim cites tier 1 or tier 2. A tier 3 page may point to a tier 1 page; cite the tier 1 page.
2. A vendor page is tier 1 for that vendor's own product, price, and terms, and marketing for any competitor.
3. Recency is not authority, and authority is not recency. A 2019 tier 1 filing loses to a 2026 tier 1 filing on a changed rule, and beats a 2026 tier 4 post.
4. Take the date from the page body or the page's own metadata. A URL segment or a search snippet is not a date.
5. An undated page can still be tier 1, but log it as `date not stated` and do not call it current.
6. A contested number needs two independent tier 1 or tier 2 sources that agree on the period and the unit.
7. A paywalled or sign-in page that you could not open is not a source. Record the blocker.
