# Brief shapes

Three shapes. Choose one, then use its headings exactly and in order. Default cap: 400 words, ten
table rows, seven list items.

## Recommendation brief

Use when one option is already preferred and the reader must approve it.

1. Decision: one sentence, the choice and its deadline.
2. Why now: three bullets, each with a citation.
3. Options: table of four rows at most, columns Option, Cost, Risk, Source.
4. What changes if approved: three bullets, concrete.
5. Recommendation: one paragraph, no hedging.
6. Next action: owner, date, source.

## Comparison brief

Use when the reader has not chosen, or asks which is better.

1. Question: what is compared and on which criteria.
2. Matrix: ten rows at most, columns Option, Criterion, Value, Unit, Source.
3. Where sources disagree: both figures, both sources, which is newer.
4. What would change the answer: the one measurement or event.
5. Recommendation: one paragraph with the condition that would reverse it.
6. Next action: owner, date, source.

## Decision memo

Use when the reader must record the choice and its cost.

1. Context: two sentences, no history.
2. Decision: one sentence.
3. Evidence: table with Claim, Value, Unit, Period, Source.
4. Cost and effort: a range with its basis, or `not found`.
5. Reversibility: reversible, costly to reverse, or final.
6. Recommendation and next action: owner, date, source.

## Shape rules

- No preamble and no closing courtesy line.
- Every bullet carries a fact, not a topic name.
- A heading with no fact gets removed, not filled.
- When a fact is missing, write `not found` in the cell and add it to the could-not-verify list.
