# Citation format

## In prose

`[Page title](url) (retrieved 2026-11-01)`

- Link text is the page's own title, trimmed to one line. Never `source`, `here`, or `link`.
- The URL is the page you opened, with no tracking parameters, no search redirect, and no fragment unless it names the section you used.
- The retrieval date is the date you opened the page, as `YYYY-MM-DD`.
- The printed publication or update date, when the page shows one, belongs in the evidence table, not in the prose.
- Cite at most five sources in prose. The table carries the rest.

## In the evidence table

Use columns Claim, Source, Printed date, Retrieved, Tier. The Source cell holds the same link form.

## Rules

1. Unopened means uncited. A source you could not open is named as a blocker, not cited.
2. Uncited means `not found`. A claim with no source never appears as a normal claim.
3. Two sources disagree: show both rows, state which page is newer, and do not average them.
4. A quote keeps the source's words inside quotation marks, ten words at most, with a section or page anchor when the source numbers them.
5. A translated quote is a quote: give the original and say who translated it.
6. A source that quotes another source is tier 3 for that fact. Find the origin or label it weak.
7. A number keeps the source's unit, currency, basis, and period. Convert only when the brief needs one unit, and show both.
8. A link to a page that changed after retrieval is still valid; the retrieval date records what was read.
9. Never build a citation from a search-result snippet, a URL slug, or a file name.
10. Never cite the evidence table itself, the draft, or this skill.
